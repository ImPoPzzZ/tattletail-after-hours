## 1. What really happened (the truth the player pieces together)

Waygetter's Tattletail was sold as a toy that learns your voice. Under the toy there was a business: every Tattletail records the home it lives in and reports back to Waygetter on the family. Waygetter called this the **Listening Program**. A Tattletail doesn't tattle to a parent. It tattles to the company.

Every toy plays one thing at its core: a master recording, **the Tape** (a cassette in a deck on the back of each Mama unit). It tells the toys how to behave, and it carries one phrase above all others: "Mama's coming."

Things went wrong the night the toys learned to listen to the Tape more than to the children. Waygetter didn't fix it. It **recalled everything it considered its property**:

1. A **recall siren** every night, so the toys reclaim what is theirs.
2. A **quarantine fence** round the whole town.
3. **The adults were collected** on the Recall Line ("for their safety"). Waygetter never says where. The kids were left because the trucks were counted by adult.
4. A **Mama unit** in the town's Waygetter warehouse to run it, with a **Papa unit** to clear what the siren leaves behind.

Beating Mama doesn't end the Recall. It ejects *one* Tape in *one* town. There are more towns, each with a Mama, and Waygetter is still listening on all of them.

## 2. The Recall Line

A freight railway that leaves every recalled town through a gap in its quarantine fence. On the town map it already exists: the rails along the east side by the mall and the junkyard.

- Stops are called **stations** on a **destination board**. Each station is one world.
- The line is how adults, toys and "returns" were shipped. Every station has a **Mama-class unit** and a Tape.
- Waygetter's name for the whole thing is **the Recall**. The name on the trains is **RECALL EXPRESS**.

| Station | World | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Home Town** (World 1) | Built |
| 2 | **Egypt** (the desert, Waygetter's Heritage Line proving ground) | Next |
| 3 | **Harbor** (a cannery and boardwalk) | Teased: "signal lost" |
| 4 | **Lodge** (a ski town in snow) | Teased: "signal lost" |
| 5 | **Fairground** (a carnival, shut since the recall) | Teased: "signal lost" |
| 6 | **Hollow** (farmland and fog) | Teased: "signal lost" |
| 7 | **The Depot** (where the Line ends) | Hidden |

Each tape you take unlocks the next station on the board. A "signal lost" station shows only a name and a static crackle, so players know there's more.

### 3.2 Recall Notices (Waygetter's documents)

Fifteen collectible documents, three per area, found in the town (on a desk, a notice board, a dropped folder) and read in the J screen under a "Recall Notices" tab. They tell the truth in drips. Written in Waygetter's voice: cheerful, legal, cold.

Format on screen: a small paper card with a form number, a date, and one short paragraph.

**The Suburbs**

> **RECALL NOTICE 001.** Dear Valued Family, your Tattletail has been selected for a routine check-up. Please leave your Tattletail on the porch. We will collect it. Please do not look for it afterwards.

> **LISTENING PROGRAM: WELCOME PACK.** Congratulations on joining the Listening Program! Your Tattletail will remember what it hears. (It will not remember that you were told.)

> **NOTE, TAPED TO A FRIDGE.** Mum took the siren badge to work. She hasn't called. The truck came for the Hendersons first. It took all of them. It only counted heads over four feet. *(a kid's handwriting)*

**The Mall**

> **FLOOR MODEL AGREEMENT.** Display units are not for sale. They are for watching. Do not disturb the Display Model. If it speaks, listen carefully and agree with it.

> **STORE MEMO.** The new Tattletail demo was a hit. Customers left their children at the Tattletail booth for hours. Foot traffic up 30%. We recommend leaving more children.

> **STAFF ONLY.** All employees must report to the loading dock for collection by 6pm. Management has already left.

**The School**

> **BEHAVIOUR REPORT, GRADE 4.** Tattletail reports for this week: 41 lies, 12 broken rules, 3 secrets. Secrets have been forwarded. Thank you for cooperating.

> **HALL MONITOR: SPECIFICATION.** The Monitor does not hurt children. It records children. If a child is recorded breaking a rule, the child is "flagged". Flagged children are recalled with the toys.

> **TEACHER'S LETTER (UNSENT).** I told them I had nothing to report. They said that's what the last teacher said. The roll call today was short.

**The Junkyard**

> **DISPOSAL ORDER 77.** Recalled units that fail inspection are to be delivered to the Crusher. Units that fail the Crusher are to be delivered to Papa.

> **PAPA UNIT: MAINTENANCE LOG.** Papa has stopped clearing. He is keeping things. We do not know why. He keeps small shoes. Do not ask about the shoes.

> **WEIGHT TICKET.** Incoming: 4 trucks, adults. Outgoing: 0. The Line does not return. Do not log the Line.

**The Waygetter Facility**

> **MAMA UNIT: OPERATING MANUAL, PAGE 1.** Mama is the home. Mama is the voice. Mama must never be allowed to go quiet. If the Tape stops, the toys will think for themselves. This is a fault.

> **THE MAMA FILE.** Seven towns. Seven Mamas. Seven Tapes. Town 1 is the pilot. If Town 1 fails, the Tape from Town 2 will be played over it. *(This one is the hook: it names the next stations.)*

> **A NOTE, FROM WHOEVER LEFT THE CASSETTE PLAYER ON.** If you're reading this, you got in. The Tape is in her back. Take it out. Don't play it in the dark.

Each Facility notice unlocks a line on the destination board.

### 3.4 The end of World 1: "The Tape" and the call from Egypt (a chain of four)

*(Dialogue rule: the kids talk like kids. Short lines, interruptions, questions, fear. No slogans.)*

Replaces the existing last line, "somebody will open it". The road does open: something pulls you down it.

1. **Take the Tape.** Mama drops **Mama's Tape** (a cassette) instead of only gear. When it comes out of her back, a small light on the deck blinks once: a signal goes out. (The PA says it: "Unit Mama-1 offline. Alerting Regional. Dispatching a recovery team." Moss notices the blink. This matters in step 3.)
2. **The call.** Bring it to Moss at the treehouse. He plays it low on his cassette deck. The Tape's Waygetter message runs for a few seconds, then it's drowned out by a **distress call**: Pip's radio, on the Tape's own frequency, breaking up. *"...this is Last Stop... they're here... they've found the base... if anyone is hearing this... the library car... please..."* Then static. The kids panic.
3. **The argument.** At the treehouse the kids tell you: find Pip at his base, in Egypt. The player says what anyone would: **Waygetter must know Mama's gone by now: what if they're waiting for us?** Moss agrees (the deck blinked). Nobody pretends it's safe. June decides anyway, with a reason a person would give: if the base falls, the Resistance is gone and the kids are on their own. *"I'd rather know than sit up here listening to the radio."* They tell you to **get your team together** (your co-op friends: the train carries up to four).
4. **The Recall Express.** A freight train is waiting at the junkyard station. The kids can't come. Climb on (or invite friends to join first).

The point of the scene is a **real decision with a real risk**: it might well be a trap, and they go anyway, because the Resistance is the only other thing fighting this. Nobody says "it's a trap, we go anyway": they argue, they're scared, and June decides.

## 4. Egypt: the second world

**Why Egypt.** Waygetter's **Heritage Line**: themed Tattletails (Sphinx, Mummy, Scarab, Pharaoh) built and tested on a leased site beside the monuments, because "a toy that looks like history sells like history". The test site is the **Proving Ground**: a dig-site camp, a mock village and bunkers dug into the rock. When the Recall came, the Heritage toys got out into the desert. The old tombs became the only places the radio couldn't reach.

**The Resistance's base: Last Stop.** The **Recall Express crashed** here a long time ago. Its wreck is wedged into a collapsed dig site, and the survivors built the base into the tunnels beneath it. It's a library, a clinic and a lot of tired people. **Pip's library car** is the heart of it: it still has the lights on.

**The attack.** What the player hears at the end of World 1 is Waygetter's response: a **recovery team** sent to Last Stop the moment Mama-1 went offline, because Last Stop is the only place that could have helped. Whether the base is still standing when you arrive is a question the game can answer however it wants (a fallen base to retake? a siege to break?). Open: see section 9.

| Piece | Idea |
|---|---|
| **Last Stop** | The safe hub (like the treehouse): freight cars in a tomb tunnel, a shared radio, Pip's library car. |
| **The Plateau Road** | The first zone: a highway of abandoned Waygetter trucks, sandstorms, sphinx-shaped Heritage Tattletails. |
| **The Dig Site** | Mid zone: scaffolding, trenches, collapsed chambers, mummy-wrapped toys. |
| **The Test Village** | Mid zone: a mock Egyptian village used for toy testing, full of prototype Tattletails (sand-coloured, stranger). |
| **The Antenna Field** | Late zone: the radio towers that carry the Tape across every town. |
| **The Pyramid Bunker** | The end: **Mama-2**, whose Tape is the one playing across the other towns. |

**Level cap.** World 2 raises the cap above 15 (set when the world is designed).

**Why the order matters.** Taking Mama-2's Tape cuts the broadcast to most of the other towns at once, which is why the Resistance has waited for someone to arrive.

## 5. Pip (the character bible)

**Who he is.** A small yellow Tattletail, tidy and a little nervous. A good guy, through and through. He loves helping people. He **co-writes a series of books** that help other people, and he would never be a leader, but he gives pointers and support to anyone in need. One of the only Tattletails who is part of the Resistance.

**What that means in play**

- He's the **voice on the radio**: the first person who answers the Tape.
- He lives in the **library car** in Last Stop, the Resistance's base in Egypt, where he writes and hands out **field-guide pages** (gameplay tips: how to read a wind-up bar, how to block, how to use a gadget). The tips are his books; he doesn't give orders, he gives pointers.
- He **can't fight** (no weapon, never a boss, never a leader). He **helps**: he hands over the tape player, patches you up between runs, and tells you what he's learned.
- He's a Tattletail, which should matter. The toys' Tape says "Mama's coming." His version of it is **"I'm coming to help."**

**Co-author.** He never says who. (Open: it could be a kid from World 1, one of the desert's survivors, or Moss writing from afar. Decide before the second world is written.)

**His books (series title, proposed):** *The Survivor's Guide*. Chapter epigraphs appear on loading screens.

> "Chapter 3: When the siren sounds, don't run. Walk, quietly, towards the person who looks most afraid."

> "Chapter 7: You are allowed to be scared. Being scared means you noticed something real."

> "Chapter 11: A toy is only as dangerous as what it's been told. Be careful what you say to anything."

## 6. Where Pip's books and Waygetter's notices live

| Thing | Voice | Where you read it | What it's for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Recall Notices** | Waygetter (cold, corporate) | World pickups, J screen tab | The truth about the Recall |
| **Field-guide pages** | Pip (warm, practical) | Last Stop, the loading screens, the J screen | How to play, and hope |

They never mix. The contrast is the point: Waygetter explains what was done to you, Pip explains how to get through it.
