# PART 1. HOW THE GAME WORKS

## 1.1 What Recalled is

A third-person co-op game for one to four players, inside Tattletail: After Hours. The toys Waygetter recalled took over a whole town at night, and the adults are gone. You're a kid fighting back with gear you craft from the toys' own parts.

You get there through **a door in the Arcade**, on the wall right behind where you arrive. Hold to use it, then choose:
- **Go out alone**
- **Invite friends**: Steam's invite list opens right there. Up to three friends can join.
- **Go out online**: you're put in a town with other players near your own progress; if there's none, your town opens and they drop in.

Recalled has its own save, its own progress and its own currencies. It shares nothing with Endless, Versus or the other modes.

## 1.2 Controls

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| WASD | Move |
| Shift | Run (loud: toys hear it) |
| Space | Jump |
| Alt (or C) | Dodge roll |
| F | Flashlight (toys see you from further with it on) |
| Left click | Attack. Hold it after a swing to wind up a heavy swing; hold it with a slingshot or blaster to draw back, release to fire |
| Right click (hold) | Block with your weapon |
| 1 / 2 / Q | Switch weapon |
| E | Use (ladders, the bench, the quest board, the stash, scrap piles, gates) |
| Tab | Backpack |
| J | The objective screen: the job you're on, every step spelled out, how far along you are, and which way it is |
| M | Map |
| P | Friends screen (invite, find players online, leave) |
| G | Mark what you're looking at (everyone sees it) |
| 5 6 7 8 | Say something ("Over here!", "Help!", "Thanks!", "Wait.") |
| H | Show the controls |

The screen also has an **arrow at the top** that always points the way you'd walk to your next objective.

## 1.3 Combat

- **Every toy winds up before it hits**, and an **orange bar** fills over its head while it does. Roll out of the way, or block.
- **Block** (right click, hold): a blue bubble of light comes up round you. A hit from the front does about a quarter of its damage. After lowering a guard you can't raise it again at once, so tapping for perfect blocks doesn't work.
- **Perfect block**: press block *just before* a hit lands (a 0.3 second window; the bubble gleams brighter for that moment). No damage, the toy is thrown off balance, and your next blow within 3 seconds is a **counter** that hits 1.6 times as hard.
- **Dodge roll** (Alt): a forward somersault, about 3.8 meters. You can't be hit for most of the roll.
- **Heavy swing**: hold attack after a swing to wind up; it hits up to twice as hard.
- **Ranged weapons**: hold to draw, release to fire. A full draw hits hardest; a quick tap does nothing.
- **Gadgets** are your second kind of weapon: a Shock Battery shocks every toy round you, a Repair Kit heals you, a Decoy Toy draws toys away, a Lid Shield soaks up damage. Each has a cooldown.
- **Noise.** Running is loud and walking isn't. Weapons make noise too (a Nail Gun is very loud; a bat is quiet). Toys hear anything within a distance and come.
- **Toys' damage** is multiplied by 0.65 (they were too much for a kid); damage never scales with the number of players, but their health does.

## 1.4 The town

A town at night with five areas, one boss each, and a safe park in the middle.

| Area | Role |
|---|---|
| **The park and the treehouse** | The one safe place. Nothing follows you into the park, and the treehouse mends you fast. It has three decks, a crafting bench, the quest board and your stash chest. You climb the oak by a ladder (hold W to climb, S to come down). |
| **The Suburbs** | The first area. Weak strays, the jack-in-the-box in the garages, Patches under the water tower. Always open. |
| **The Mall** | Two floors of dead shops round an atrium, a parking lot full of cars with RC cars patrolling it. The Display Model. |
| **The School** | Dark halls, a second floor, a boiler room under it. Hall Robots patrol it. The Hall Monitor in the gym. |
| **The Junkyard** | The best scrap and the worst toys: Gold Tattletails, Fused toys. Rails run through it to a station. Papa on his throne of scrap. |
| **The Waygetter Facility** | The last area: the real factory (it's built by the game's own factory code). Mama hunts you through it before her fight. |

- **The way in.** Each area's barricade (a fence of planks and recall tape the kids built) opens when you're the right level **and** you've beaten the boss before it.
- **The streets.** Main Street runs north from the Arcade's door to the park. Side roads lead to each area.
- **The towns' zones build as you near them** and go away as you leave, so the town loads fast.
- **The Forts.** When you hand in the kids' first job in an area, they build a fort there with a **zipline** back to the treehouse. (It builds by itself when you hand the job in.)
- **Scrap piles.** Heaps of junk you can search with E. Each gives 2 to 4 rolls of materials plus batteries (1 or 2 in the suburbs, 2 or 3 beyond). A searched pile has more **after 3 minutes**, and it tells you how long.
- **Zone moods.** Each area has its own fog and light: cold blue suburbs, sick-green mall, dried-blood school, rust-brown junkyard, close and cold Facility. The fog thickens when toys are on you.

## 1.5 The toys

- **Strays** (Tattletails in colour tiers: purple, yellow, teal, black, white, scrapyard, warehouse; and Gold and Diamond ones that are rare elites) come in **packs of 2 to 4**, and the rest of a pack comes when one notices you.
- **Other toys, one trick each:** the **Jack-in-the-Box** (can't move, bursts every 2 seconds in a red ring), the **RC Car** (fast, rams you and comes round again), the **Hall Robot** (shoots), the **Fused Toy** (welded together, tough).
- **Sight and hearing.** A toy sees you at about 11 meters (a boss 30), further with your flashlight on. They also hear noise. You can slip past a pack by walking in the dark.
- **They don't go through ceilings.** Different floors count as much further apart.
- **Mama hunts you** through the Facility before her fight, and can hear you. Hide when you hear her.
- **A boss fight belongs to whoever was there when it began.** Anyone who turns up later waits outside until it's over. After it falls, the boss comes back (about 15 seconds later) as long as anyone in the town hasn't beaten it, so a friend can still do it.

## 1.6 Levels, health and armor

- **Levels 1 to 15** (World 1's cap). Every level past the first adds **30 health**. Levels come from experience: toys give it, jobs pay it, bosses give about a third of a level.
- **Armor** is armor points: each point is **1 percent less damage from everything**.
- **Gear needs a level** to equip (it can be carried below that).
- **Boss parts** (Patches' Thread, the Display Glass, the Monitor Lens, the Monocle) each make a **bigger backpack** at the bench (+5 slots each, four upgrades). Mama's Eye is gear material. The bench says exactly what an item does before you make it.
- **Backpack:** 20 slots to start, 100 slots in the **stash chest**, 50 to a stack.

## 1.7 Crafting

At the treehouse bench (first deck): you pick an item, read what it does, and press MAKE IT. The bench uses your backpack and your stash **together**. Better recipes unlock as you beat bosses (the bench tier is how many bosses you've beaten). Materials come from toys and from scrap piles. The full table is in Part 2.

## 1.8 Dying

If you go down, a friend can help you up (press E beside them). If nobody does, you wake in the treehouse after a few seconds, and the materials you were carrying **stay where you fell** in a bag you can go back for. You don't lose levels, gear or jobs.

## 1.9 Playing with friends

- **Up to four players** in one town. The **host** (the player whose lobby it is) runs the toys; if they leave, another player takes over.
- **Everyone keeps their own character, level and loot.** Toys get tougher with the group (x1, x1.6, x2.2, x2.8 health for 1 to 4 players); damage doesn't scale; experience isn't split.
- **Credit.** A toy that breaks near you pays you its experience, its loot roll (your own) and counts toward your job, whoever struck the last blow. A boss pays everyone in its area.
- **The same job (new in 3.6).** In a friend's town you all work on **the host's job** together: kills, crafts and pickups count for it, and each of you hands it in at the board for your own pay. Your own saved job and its progress are never touched, and are exactly where you left them when you leave. If the host is on a job you've already done, you help but there's nothing to hand in.
- **Trading.** GIVE in the backpack hands an item to the friend beside you.
- **The friends screen (P)** shows who's in town, their level and their job. A friends list shows each player's job.
- **Version.** Everyone in a town needs the same After Hours version (3.6 Early Access).
- The game checks a newcomer's character: one that couldn't have been earned (a level-13 weapon at level 1) is turned away.

## 1.10 Saves

Recalled saves to its own protected file in Documents\Tattletail After Hours: encrypted and signed with your Steam account (or your PC without Steam), so it can't be edited or copied between accounts. A rejected file is never silently thrown away: the last backup is tried, and a copy of the bad one is kept.

New in 3.6: **your best progress is kept in its own file**. Experience and bosses only go up in Recalled, so a save with less progress than the best one is replaced by it (with a note: "Your progress was recovered from a backup"). The Recalled save is never changed by anything a friend does.

## 1.12 The music and the feel

Every area has three music layers (exploring, tense, fighting), the treehouse has a lullaby, and Mama has her own theme. The music follows the danger: quiet until toys notice you, then tense, then a fight. The treehouse is the one place with silence and warmth.

## The jobs (the quest chain)
### 1. The Treehouse  (Talk, The Suburbs)
- Board: "Find the treehouse in the park, up Main Street, and talk to the kids."
- Done: "You made it. Most don't. Take this, you'll need it out there."
- Pays: 40 experience, 1 Baseball Bat, 1 Pan Lid
- How:
  1. You start outside the Arcade, on Main Street. Walk north (the way you're facing when you arrive) along the lit road until you reach the park.
  2. The big oak in the middle of the park is the treehouse. Nothing follows you into the park.
  3. Go to the ladder on the trunk and press E to grab it. Hold W to climb; you step off on the first deck.
  4. Walk up to the quest board (the plank sign beside the kids, in the far right corner of the deck) and press E.
  5. Press HAND IT IN. They give you a Baseball Bat and a Pan Lid.
  6. Press TAB to open your backpack, click the bat, press EQUIP. Do the same for the lid.

### 2. Strays  (Kill, The Suburbs, 8)
- Board: "The suburbs are full of strays. Break 8 toys there."
- Done: "That's eight fewer on Maple Street. They hear everything, so stay off the grass when you can."
- Pays: 110 experience, 4 Spring, 4 Plastic
- How:
  1. Grab the ladder, hold S to climb down, and leave the park by the south path, back down Main Street.
  2. Maple Street is the side street on the right (the west side), a little before you get back to the Arcade. The gap in the plank fence is the way into the suburbs.
  3. Break 8 toys. Any toy in the suburbs counts.
  4. Left click swings your bat. Keep it held after a swing to wind up a heavy swing, then let go: it hits up to twice as hard.
  5. Every toy winds up before it hits, and an orange bar fills over its head while it does. Roll out of the way with ALT (or C): you're untouchable for most of the roll.
  6. Or hold right click to block with your weapon: a blue bubble of light comes up, and a hit from the front does a quarter of its damage. Raise it just before the hit lands (the bubble gleams brighter for that moment) for a perfect block: no damage at all, the toy is knocked off balance, and your next blow counters for more.
  7. Strays come in packs of 2 to 4, and running (SHIFT) is loud. Walk up to one pack at a time.
  8. When it says 8 / 8, go back to the treehouse and hand it in at the quest board.

### 3. Make Something  (Craft, The Suburbs)
- Board: "Bring parts back to the bench and craft a Slingshot."
- Done: "Loud, isn't it? Everything in the street heard that."
- Pays: 110 experience, 4 Screws
- How:
  1. A Slingshot costs 4 Springs and 3 Plastic. The last job paid you 4 of each, so you should already have them.
  2. If you're short: strays drop both, and the scrap piles in the suburbs (heaps of junk: press E to search one) give both. A searched pile has more after 3 minutes; it tells you how long.
  3. In the treehouse, first deck: the crafting bench is on the left side. Press E at it. It uses the materials in your stash too.
  4. Click Slingshot in the list.
  5. Press TAB, click the Slingshot, press EQUIP. It goes in your second weapon slot: press 2 (or Q) to take it out.
  6. To fire it: hold left click to draw it back, aim with the dot in the middle of the screen, let go. A full draw hits hardest; a quick tap does nothing.
  7. Hand the job in at the quest board.

### 4. The Garages  (Elite, The Suburbs)
- Board: "Something in the garages on Oak Lane jumps out at people. Break it."
- Done: "A jack-in-the-box. Figures. Keep the spring."
- Pays: 130 experience, 1 Shock Battery
- How:
  1. Go into the suburbs and follow Maple Street west to the lane that runs south (left), about halfway along. It leads down to Oak Lane.
  2. On Oak Lane turn east (left) and walk to its end. The row of four concrete garages is on the south side.
  3. Two of the garages have a red box with a gold crank sitting in them. That is the Jack-in-the-Box. It pops when you come within about 3 meters.
  4. It can't move. Every 2 seconds it bursts and hurts everything within 4 meters: a red ring shows where.
  5. Easiest way: stand outside the garage, more than 5 meters off, and shoot it with the Slingshot. Or run in, swing twice, and back out before the burst.
  6. Break one, then hand the job in at the treehouse. The reward is a Shock Battery (a gadget: equip it in a weapon slot, click to set it off).

### 5. Patches  (Boss, The Suburbs)
- Board: "A huge thing made of other Tattletails sits in the cul-de-sac under the water tower. It has to go."
- Done: "You broke Patches. The mall's barricade is open to you now."
- Pays: what it drops, 1 Lucky Marble
- How:
  1. You need to be level 4 or close to it: Patches has 900 health and hits for 14. Make a Pan Lid and better gear at the bench first if you haven't.
  2. Go down Oak Lane to its west end. The cul-de-sac under the water tower (the tall tank on legs you can see from anywhere in the suburbs) is where it sits.
  3. Its health bar appears at the top of the screen when the fight starts. Leave the cul-de-sac and it gives up and heals.
  4. When a red ring appears on the ground around it, get out of the ring: a second later it slams, and a slam can't be blocked.
  5. At two thirds and one third health it calls 3 strays. Break them first, or block with right click while you deal with them.
  6. When it breaks it drops Patches' Thread (for a bigger backpack, at the bench) and a piece of gear. The mall's barricade opens.
  7. Hand the job in at the treehouse.

### 6. Shoplifters  (Kill, The Mall, 12)
- Board: "Clear out the mall. Break 12 toys there."
- Done: "The mall used to be where we got batteries. Now we can again."
- Pays: 250 experience, 6 Battery
- How:
  1. The mall is east of the park. Leave the park by the east path and follow the road to its end: the barricade there opens once Patches is broken and you're level 4.

## 1. What really happened (the truth the player pieces together)

Waygetter's Tattletail was sold as a toy that learns your voice. Under the toy there was a business: every Tattletail records the home it lives in and reports back to Waygetter on the family. Waygetter called this the **Listening Program**. A Tattletail doesn't tattle to a parent. It tattles to the company.

Every toy plays one thing at its core: a master recording, **the Tape** (a cassette in a deck on the back of each Mama unit). It tells the toys how to behave, and it carries one phrase above all others: "Mama's coming."

Things went wrong the night the toys learned to listen to the Tape more than to the children. Waygetter didn't fix it. It **recalled everything it considered its property**:

1. A **recall siren** every night, so the toys reclaim what is theirs.
2. A **quarantine fence** round the whole town.
3. **The adults were collected** on the Recall Line ("for their safety"). Waygetter never says where. The kids were left because the trucks were counted by adult.
4. A **Mama unit** in the town's Waygetter warehouse to run it, with a **Papa unit** to clear what the siren leaves behind.

Beating Mama doesn't end the Recall. It ejects *one* Tape in *one* town. There are more towns, each with a Mama, and Waygetter is still listening on all of them.

## 2. The Recall Line

A freight railway that leaves every recalled town through a gap in its quarantine fence. On the town map it already exists: the rails along the east side by the mall and the junkyard.

- Stops are called **stations** on a **destination board**. Each station is one world.
- The line is how adults, toys and "returns" were shipped. Every station has a **Mama-class unit** and a Tape.
- Waygetter's name for the whole thing is **the Recall**. The name on the trains is **RECALL EXPRESS**.

| Station | World | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Home Town** (World 1) | Built |
| 2 | **Egypt** (the desert, Waygetter's Heritage Line proving ground) | Next |
| 3 | **Harbor** (a cannery and boardwalk) | Teased: "signal lost" |
| 4 | **Lodge** (a ski town in snow) | Teased: "signal lost" |
| 5 | **Fairground** (a carnival, shut since the recall) | Teased: "signal lost" |
| 6 | **Hollow** (farmland and fog) | Teased: "signal lost" |
| 7 | **The Depot** (where the Line ends) | Hidden |

Each tape you take unlocks the next station on the board. A "signal lost" station shows only a name and a static crackle, so players know there's more.

## 4. Egypt: the second world

**Why Egypt.** Waygetter's **Heritage Line**: themed Tattletails (Sphinx, Mummy, Scarab, Pharaoh) built and tested on a leased site beside the monuments, because "a toy that looks like history sells like history". The test site is the **Proving Ground**: a dig-site camp, a mock village and bunkers dug into the rock. When the Recall came, the Heritage toys got out into the desert. The old tombs became the only places the radio couldn't reach.

**The Resistance's base: Last Stop.** The **Recall Express crashed** here a long time ago. Its wreck is wedged into a collapsed dig site, and the survivors built the base into the tunnels beneath it. It's a library, a clinic and a lot of tired people. **Pip's library car** is the heart of it: it still has the lights on.

**The attack.** What the player hears at the end of World 1 is Waygetter's response: a **recovery team** sent to Last Stop the moment Mama-1 went offline, because Last Stop is the only place that could have helped. Whether the base is still standing when you arrive is a question the game can answer however it wants (a fallen base to retake? a siege to break?). Open: see section 9.

## 5. Pip (the character bible)

**Who he is.** A small yellow Tattletail, tidy and a little nervous. A good guy, through and through. He loves helping people. He **co-writes a series of books** that help other people, and he would never be a leader, but he gives pointers and support to anyone in need. One of the only Tattletails who is part of the Resistance.

**What that means in play**

- He's the **voice on the radio**: the first person who answers the Tape.
- He lives in the **library car** in Last Stop, the Resistance's base in Egypt, where he writes and hands out **field-guide pages** (gameplay tips: how to read a wind-up bar, how to block, how to use a gadget). The tips are his books; he doesn't give orders, he gives pointers.
- He **can't fight** (no weapon, never a boss, never a leader). He **helps**: he hands over the tape player, patches you up between runs, and tells you what he's learned.
- He's a Tattletail, which should matter. The toys' Tape says "Mama's coming." His version of it is **"I'm coming to help."**
